# Tax-Efficient Withdrawals

Tax-efficient withdrawal planning decides which accounts and assets to draw on, in what order and in what amounts, so a client's retirement income lasts as long as possible after tax - often adding as much value as a better investment return.

## Why It Matters

<div class="audience-beginner" markdown="1">

When you stop working, you start living off your savings. The same money can be taken out in ways that cost very different amounts of tax. Taking it from the right account, in the right order, can make savings last years longer - without earning a single extra rupee or dollar of return.

</div>

<div class="audience-analyst" markdown="1">

Decumulation is a multi-period optimization under tax brackets that are progressive and partly indexed, with account-level rules (RMDs, exemptions, holding periods). The objective is to minimize lifetime (and legacy) tax, not this year's tax - often by deliberately realizing income in low-bracket years. Sequence-of-returns risk adds a second constraint: withdrawals from a falling portfolio permanently impair it, so the asset drawn from matters as much as the account.

</div>

## Sequence-of-Returns Risk

Two retirees earn the same average return over 30 years, but one suffers a crash in year 1 and the other in year 25. Withdrawing during the early crash sells assets at low prices, and the portfolio may never recover. The order of returns matters once money is flowing out.

The **4% rule** (Bengen, 1994) found that, in US history, withdrawing 4% of the starting portfolio (then raising it with inflation) lasted at least 30 years for a stock-bond portfolio. It is a starting point, not a guarantee - lower for longer retirements, higher-fee portfolios, or markets with weaker history.

## United States

### Account types

| Type | Examples | Tax on withdrawal |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable | Brokerage account | Only on gains, dividends and interest; long-term gains at 0/15/20% |
| Tax-deferred | Traditional 401(k), traditional IRA | Ordinary income; required minimum distributions (RMDs) from age 73 (75 for those born 1960 or later) |
| Tax-free | Roth IRA, Roth 401(k) | None, if qualified; no lifetime RMDs for Roth IRAs |
| HSA | Health savings account | Tax-free for qualified medical expenses |

### A withdrawal order

The conventional order - **taxable first, then tax-deferred, then Roth** - lets tax-advantaged accounts compound longest. Better plans usually blend the accounts to keep taxable income steady across years:

1. **Early retirement (before Social Security and RMDs)** - live mostly on taxable accounts, and use the low-income years to **convert traditional IRA money to Roth** up to the top of a low bracket (for example, the 12% bracket), and to **harvest long-term gains at the 0% rate** where possible.
2. **From RMD age** - take RMDs (required), top up from taxable accounts, and keep Roth assets for late-life spending, large one-off expenses or heirs.
3. **Charitable clients** - from age 70½, qualified charitable distributions (QCDs) from an IRA can satisfy RMDs without adding to taxable income.

**Asset location:** hold tax-inefficient assets (taxable bonds, REITs) in tax-deferred accounts, and tax-efficient assets (broad equity index ETFs) in taxable accounts; put the highest-growth assets in Roth accounts.

## India

### Instruments and tax (2025-26)

| Source | Tax treatment |
|---|---|
| Equity mutual funds / listed shares (held over 12 months) | LTCG at 12.5% on gains above 1.25 lakh rupees a year |
| Equity SWP (systematic withdrawal plan) | Only the **gain portion** of each redemption is taxed |
| Debt mutual funds (bought from April 2023) | Gains taxed at slab rate |
| Bank FDs, Post Office schemes, SCSS | Interest taxed at slab rate |
| EPF (after 5 years of continuous service) | Withdrawal tax-free (interest on large employee contributions can be taxable) |
| PPF | Tax-free (EEE) |
| NPS at 60 | Traditionally up to 60% of the corpus as a tax-free lump sum, with at least 40% used to buy an annuity whose income is taxed at slab rate (PFRDA has been revising exit rules - check the current limits) |

### Why an SWP from equity can beat FD interest

A retiree needs 6 lakh rupees a year. Compare (ignoring surcharge and cess, and assuming a 30% slab rate for the FD case):

| | FD interest of 6 lakh | Equity SWP of 6 lakh with 50% embedded gain |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable amount | 6,00,000 | Gain: 3,00,000 - exemption 1,25,000 = 1,75,000 |
| Tax rate | 30% slab | 12.5% |
| Tax | **1,80,000** | **21,875** |
| Effective tax on withdrawal | 30% | 3.6% |

The SWP keeps more after tax because most of each withdrawal is return of capital, and gains are taxed at a lower rate with an annual exemption. The trade-off is market risk, which is why the bucket strategy keeps near-term spending in debt.

### Tactics for Indian retirees

- **Harvest equity LTCG up to 1.25 lakh each year** (sell and rebuy) to reset cost basis tax-free.
- **Choose between the old and new tax regimes** each year - the new regime (default since 2023-24) has lower rates but removes most deductions such as 80C and 80TTB.
- **Stagger FD maturities** and use debt funds or target-maturity funds to manage when income is recognized.
- **Use the family** - spouse and parents with lower income can hold investments (mind the clubbing provisions for gifts to a spouse).

## Red Flags

- Withdrawing everything from one account type because it is "simplest."
- Large IRA balances left untouched until RMDs push the client into a high bracket.
- Retirees living entirely on FD interest in the highest slab.
- No cash buffer, so equities are sold in every downturn.
- Ignoring the tax cost of rebalancing in taxable accounts.

## Case Studies

**US - the RMD tax bomb.** A couple retires at 62 with 2 million dollars in traditional IRAs and modest taxable savings. If they leave the IRAs untouched, RMDs from 73 onward, plus Social Security, can push them into a higher bracket for the rest of their lives and raise Medicare premiums through IRMAA surcharges. Partial Roth conversions from 62 to 72, when their taxable income is low, spread the tax over low-bracket years and shrink future RMDs.

**India - senior citizen income planning.** A retired couple in India with 1.5 crore rupees in FDs pays tax at the slab rate on all interest. Moving part of the money into an equity-savings or balanced-advantage fund with an SWP, while keeping 2-3 years of spending in FDs and SCSS, can cut their annual tax substantially and add inflation protection - at the cost of some volatility that the FD bucket absorbs.

## Study Notes

- **Sequence risk**: early losses during withdrawals do lasting damage; use a **cash/bond buffer**.
- **4% rule** is a historical guide, not a guarantee.
- US: blend taxable, tax-deferred and Roth withdrawals; **Roth conversions** and **0% gain harvesting** in low-income years; plan for **RMDs**.
- India: **equity SWP** taxes only gains, at 12.5% above 1.25 lakh; FD interest is taxed at slab; harvest LTCG yearly; choose the regime each year.
- Asset **location** complements asset allocation.
